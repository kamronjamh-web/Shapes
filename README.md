# Shapes

A Godot C# first-person horde-defense prototype. Shapes define base weapon identities; installed modulators customize their behavior. Enemy shapes drop run currency and occasionally weapon shapes or modulators.

Open `project.godot` in Godot .NET, enter the hub, and select **North Gate** to play the first map. The new fort layout uses a **1,609.344 × 1,609.344 m** playable square, one square mile in total. A centered **200 × 200 m** clear, open-sky base contains the core and shop at an elevation of **8 m**. Its outer walls are **20 ft / 6.096 m** high, with four cardinal entry gates **15 ft / 4.572 m** wide and **1,000 health** each by default. Outside the walls, the hill slopes down from roughly 102 m to 220 m from the center. A continuous **3 m wall walk**, **8 × 6 m** outward firing platforms, and access ramps provide elevated firing positions.

The initial wave remains **100 cubes**, split into **25 per cardinal approach**. Manual population tests distribute up to **2,000 enemies** across all four approaches. Expanded-fort validation passes **80 checks**, including player access, downward firing from all platform outer edges, and real Fort-tab health/restore controls. Rendered fort, gate, access, and assault views were inspected. The older compact-map results below do not establish this fort's performance.

- **WASD / mouse / Space:** move, look, and jump.
- **Left / right mouse:** fire / aim.
- **Q / E / mouse wheel:** switch collected weapons.
- **Tab:** open or close the paused weapon workbench.
- **F:** open or close an available shop within range.
- **N:** start the next wave during preparation.
- **Esc:** close inspection/shop or open the pause menu. Choose **Inventory / Equipment** to inspect your weapon.
- **Grave / backtick (`):** open or close live gameplay tuning; Escape closes tuning first.

The FPS **weapon workbench** pauses combat and shows searchable shape/modulator grids around an interactive 3D weapon. Drag a collected shape onto the weapon to equip immediately and watch its components fold around the shape core, then assemble into the new weapon. Clicking a shape previews it; **Equip this shape** also applies it. Drag to rotate and scroll to zoom. Rarity colors the gun accents. Drop a loose modulator on an empty physical socket or its numbered dock button; drag an installed cartridge back to inventory to remove it. Other cartridges retain their socket positions. Locked, occupied and incompatible targets reject drops, and closing cancels an unfinished drag. Stats update from the current FPS combat rules, while selecting a cartridge explains its quality and effects. Tab/Escape restores the previous combat/menu state. This replaces the FPS inventory only, uses current-run items, preserves wounded health fractions and leaves hub saves intact.

All sixteen weapon models now include Blender-authored **Disassemble / Assemble** clips (0.70 seconds each) and ten pairs of cartridge insertion/removal clips (0.40 seconds). The core stays anchored while the surrounding mechanism moves. Editable derived files live in `Art/WeaponWorkbenchAnimations`; original approved concepts and firing animations are preserved. [Art commands](Tools/Art/README.txt) explain the exports. Inventory thumbnails render once and closing disables the workbench viewports.

The gameplay tuning menu provides **100 / 500 / 1,000 / 2,000** population presets, cube or pyramid selection, and **Add / Replace / Clear** controls. Use Replace for repeatable count comparisons. Combat continues while editing; player movement, aiming, and firing are held until the menu closes. Its Simulation tab can pause gameplay or disable crowd simulation, and adjusts tick rates, separation, and player aggro live. Enemy health, speed, damage, and cooldown edits affect existing enemies as well as new spawns; wounded health fractions are preserved. Player/core invulnerability supports longer stress runs. The existing paused stat/loadout editor is linked from the Player tab.

Menu changes apply to copies of the current run's tuning resources. Restarting restores project defaults. Manual population testing holds the scripted wave flow until an encounter rebuild or restart. Clear removes enemies and old attacks without granting kill rewards; Add and Replace keep currency, equipment, and objective health. Bounded formations use four exterior spawn sectors. The **Fort** tab tunes maximum gate health from **1 to 20,000**, preserves wounded fractions and breaches, toggles gate invulnerability, and restores all gates to full health. Restoring also closes breaches. The HUD reports north/east/south/west gate HP.

Enemies make soft body contact and climb over one another under crowd pressure. Losing support makes bodies fall. The **Crowd contact** tab adjusts contact stiffness, forward push, climbing speed, height allowance, gravity, passes, and neighbor budget live. Defaults use one contact pass and a 10 m climbing allowance above terrain. Disabling climbing stops new ascent while existing bodies remain solid; disabling soft collisions restores the earlier steering separation. Raised enemies retain their real hit capsules and require actual vertical reach for melee attacks. Climbing over the enlarged fort walls and standing on its wall walk still require validation.

Cubes roll through quarter turns as they travel, with short pauses between steps. Their visible rotation follows actual movement, so blocked cubes stop rolling. Unsupported airborne groups cannot sustain each other in midair; roof-underside falling and rooftop support remain covered by the older compact fixture. The **Movement / deaths** tab controls rolling, pauses, corpse lifetime and launch strength, and base equipment drop rates.

Enemy equipment drops now have independent base chances of **0.5% for a shape** and **1% for a modulator**, before Luck bonuses. Killed crowd shapes become tumbling physics bodies launched away from the actual projectile, explosion, or other damage source. Overkill is damage beyond the victim's remaining health; greater excess increases launch speed up to a cap. The reusable corpse pool defaults to **64 bodies lasting 4 seconds**, and recycles the oldest under mass kills. Corpses collide with the environment, pause with the game, and do not block the player or receive weapon hits.

Shops appear during preparation and after enough kills during combat. Combat continues while shopping during a wave. Shops sell weapon shapes, shape levels, core repairs, and compatible modulators; recovered modulators can be installed for free. Uncollected loot is gathered at wave clear.

The catalog has 99 active modulators plus the preserved, retired Apex Doctrine definition. North Gate supports the 16 weapon transforms, critical-hit procs, survival stats, Luck, and all four Wings. Square fires moving bullets; flight speed, piercing, ricochets, and wall bounces affect real attacks. Shops rotate shapes from the full weapon catalog and offer compatible parts. Wings require a support weapon after the first arsenal slot, with one Wing per weapon and one of each Wing type per run. Incompatible or retired parts on imported hub weapons move into the run's recovered storage; saved equipment stays intact.

Modulator quality is **Broken 50% / Baseline 100% / Enhanced 150% / Flawless 200%**; Baseline names have no prefix. Tier 1/2/3 denotes effect rarity, and each can roll every quality. Normal quality odds are **60/30/9/1%**, with rarity weights **70/25/5%**. A cleared defense wave guarantees one modulator using those same odds. The field shop fuses two matching loose parts plus coins into the next quality, lets installed parts return to storage, and previews actual effects and comparisons. The final reward remains accessible through the victory workshop. FPS rewards/fusion are local to the run. [Quality rules, costs and save compatibility](Docs/ModulatorQuality.txt) describe the implementation and future skill-tree hook.

Hub inventory, run assembly, FPS shops/debug tools and weapon inspection share compatibility rules and resolved quality descriptions. The inspector explains extra seekers/mines/clones, initial-hit restrictions, Mimic's crit limits, selected-only player bonuses, and Apex retirement. Mobius repeats each original shot once. Validation passes **2,321 quality/economy/save checks**, **484 FPS modulator checks** including the **1,600-pair installation matrix**, **1,222 headless inspection checks** and **1,225 native inspection checks**. The native inspection run verifies real mouse drags and focused-Tab cancellation; the headless run explicitly skips OS hover/drag dispatch and checks the drop contracts. These checks do not establish whole-catalog balance. `RunWeaponInspectionValidation.ps1 -Native -GodotPath <path>` runs the native suite. `RunWeaponInspectionCapture.ps1 -Workbench -Resolution 1280x720 -Shapes square -GodotPath <path>` captures inventory grids, a held shape drag, both transformation phases and cartridge installation/removal. `-Modulators -QualityWorkshop -Shapes 'square,pyramid,klein,boys'` additionally captures explanations, actual fusion and the victory workshop.

The next catalog direction is to replace overlapping stat parts while retaining a small useful stat set. [The modulator impact plan](Docs/ModulatorImpactPlan.txt) proposes seven replacements, starting with a crowd-displacing Square shot, an aim-following Triangle beam and density-triggered Hexagon mines. These replacement effects are proposals and are not yet in the game.

Map dimensions, spawn count, loot rates, and shop prices have project defaults in `Resources/Fps/HillFort_*.tres`. Geometry rebuilds preserve objective health and run equipment; gate integration preserves wounded health fractions and breached states. Crowd movement, decision frequency, separation, and enemy archetypes apply live. Allocated crowd capacity is 2,000 on North Gate and requires restarting the scene to change.

Weapon effects cover **all sixteen shapes**: slugs, spinning saws, triangular beams, unfolding hex mines, nova rings, tumbling cube guards, banking seekers, faceted flames, nested rifts, arcing mortars, target-linked lightning, spinning drills, inward void spirals, delayed echo traces, health-scaled wrath boundaries, and fragmented clones. Each weapon also has distinct muzzle/insert pulses and synthesized firing/impact sounds; actual mine/mortar detonations and echo/clone attacks trigger their own cues. Existing authored mechanisms provide recoil. Damage, cooldowns, collision footprints, and modulator behavior stay independent of presentation. The grave-key menu's **Weapon effects** tab toggles polish and sound, and adjusts trail/fragment detail. Active field boundaries remain visible at low detail.

`RunWeaponPolishValidation.ps1 -GodotPath <Godot .NET executable>` checks every weapon's real-target damage and 60 Hz damage timeline with effects on/off, mine arming, delayed echoes, passive health scaling, bounded pools, pause/reset, player feedback, and audio playback/volume handling. `RunWeaponPolishCapture.ps1` writes 55 first-person/world/crowd views and native geometry checks to `.tmp/weapon-polish-capture`. It restores the same staged 2,000-enemy compact-map snapshot and runs all sixteen attacks for each effects budget, with 60 warmup and 480 sampled frames. This forced firing cadence and short VSync-limited sample are diagnostics, not expanded-fort or sustained-combat performance guarantees. Forty-nine synthesized audio samples are exported to `.tmp/weapon-polish-audio` for listening review.

The crowd uses packed enemy state, a spatial grid, and two shared MultiMesh renderers. Weapons use individual enemy handles for piercing, area attacks, homing, and modulator effects; minions do not create individual physics bodies or mesh nodes.
