LIVE DEFENSES
The field shop on the northeast middle terrace offers four turret mounts, one
per cardinal balcony. Buy a Square (one is guaranteed in each shop restock),
unlock a mount for 20 coins, and transfer an owned Square into it. Installed
modulators stay on the exact item. The player must retain one equipped shape.
Mounting frees an inventory slot; returning requires room in the five active
plus two reserve shape inventory. Full storage leaves equipment mounted.

This first turret supports Square projectiles and passive damage, cadence,
range, size, projectile speed, critical-hit, extra-projectile and pierce effects.
Unsupported installed effects explicitly block mounting; remove them in Tab
before mounting. Player-only base substats do not transfer to a turret. Triangle,
Circle and other shape turret interpretations remain future work.

Each mount covers its cardinal sector and can prioritize nearby targets or
tougher nearby targets. It has 250 HP; nearby enemies can disable it while its
equipment stays mounted. Repair costs 10 coins. Mounted shape upgrades use the
existing 40-coin price and level cap. Bullets cannot heal the player, trigger
Wings or use player-specific on-hit/on-fire effect state.

Target acquisition is staggered and uses bounded spatial sampling, capped
candidate and visibility checks, and a rotating cell cursor. Priorities apply
to observed candidates, not a global search of all 50k enemies. At most96 turret
bullets exist. This bounds work but does not establish a new FPS benchmark.

LAYOUT
Retain the 200 x 200 m courtyard footprint at world elevation 8 m. Four cardinal
approaches have 24 m clear openings. An intermediate square terrace is 122 m wide
overall, with a connected 12 m circulation ring at elevation 11 m. A 30 x 30 m
central fallback/core platform stands at elevation 14 m. Cardinal ramps rise
3 m over 18 m, with 24 m clear width. The horde can reach every objective tier.

The outer balconies reward early outward fire. The middle ring lets the player
change sectors and cover a breached front. The central platform creates a final
fallback with broad incoming ramps. Different sector landmarks aid orientation
without turning the approaches into narrow enemy queues.

DEFENSE UPGRADE CONCEPT
Four 5 x 5 m turret hardpoints sit on the outer firing balconies, one per front.
Their blockout sockets illustrate an owned shape determining the attack and
installed modulators specializing it. The shown cartridge/socket count is a
placeholder, not a change to item socket rules. Flush placement reserves show
space for slow strips and barricades, with a repair pocket off the central routes.
Turret equipment placeholders are excluded from the export and replaced with
working mounts. Slow strips, barricades, repair-pocket props, shields and blast
plates remain design placeholders; the existing shop still repairs the core.

Longer-term turret direction:
- Unlock mounts through the run shop, with four active mounts as the ceiling.
- Transfer an owned run shape and its installed modulators into the mount without
  duplicating equipment; keep at least one player weapon equipped.
- Let the player designate a sector or priority target. Turrets support the
  player's attack rather than autonomously resolving an entire front.
- Preserve mounted equipment when a turret is disabled. Returning it requires
  storage room, or an explicit ground drop following the existing loot rules.
- Start with Square projectiles; add Triangle beam and Circle saw behavior next.
  Each remaining shape needs an explicit stationary-defense interpretation.

Other defenses worth testing, in order:
1. Repair/reinforcement: make spending on holding a front compete with upgrading
   the player's weapon. Existing core repair can support the central station.
2. Short repairable barricades: create cover and retreat time, while keeping
   broad lanes instead of forcing every enemy into one tight gate.
3. Broad slow strips: create useful firing windows for player weapons/turrets,
   with modest slowing that keeps the horde moving.
4. Directional shield emitters: temporary protection for a chosen firing position.
5. Player-triggered blast plates: optional deliberate explosions driven by the
   player's shot or command, consistent with the player/weapon chain-reaction goal.

IMPLEMENTATION FINDINGS
The existing attack dispatcher accepts a weapon, origin and direction, but is not
safe to reuse wholesale for turrets: several attacks center/follow the player,
lifesteal heals the player, firing can trigger Wings, and inventory swaps clear
shared projectile/effect lists. First introduce explicit shooter context and
separate effect ownership, then enable attack families deliberately. Target
acquisition must use spatial candidates and staggered updates, not scan 50k enemies
for each turret every frame. Start with at most four active turret mounts.

Current GPU fort snapshots and spatial masks support 64 solids and 64 ramps.
The live architecture uses39 simple box proxies and16 cardinal-ramp proxies;
decorative trim and upgrade placeholders do not add collision. CollisionProxies.tres
packages the authoritative data for player physics, CPU crowd support, GPU world
snapshots and corpse worker collision. Decreasing-coordinate inward ramps reverse
their longitudinal axis to retain increasing-height CPU/GPU ramp contracts.

Four24m entrances remain open without gate barriers. Stable per-slot lateral
targets distribute approaches across the ramp width. CPU and GPU steering use
matching stages through the outer ramp, middle link, inner ramp and raised core.
Player/core/shop anchors now use platform-aware grounding. Legacy compact and
gated-fort fixtures explicitly preserve their former geometry for regression tests.

The default defense resource currently provides one 100-enemy wave. Its expected
spare shape drops are below one item per wave, so turret availability should use
the existing shape shop rather than require a lucky drop. Longer upgrade progression
will need additional waves and a measured economy pass.

The Blender source was reopened and checked for finite geometry and proxy counts.
The integrated map is playable; sustained50k combat and older-GPU FPS still need
measurement. RunSteppedStrongholdValidation.ps1 covers actual physics walkability,
shared supports, native CPU/GPU crowd routes, rebuild preservation and screenshots.
RunFortTurretValidation.ps1 covers shop purchase/unlock, exact equipment ownership,
projectile damage, repair and inventory capacity. Existing gated-fort regressions
use LegacyExpandedFortFixture instead of depending on the new default map.
